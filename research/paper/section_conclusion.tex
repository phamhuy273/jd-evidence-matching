\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
In this ERA paper, we presented a controlled $2 \times 2$ factorial evaluation crossing chunking strategy with context headers across 40 repositories. 
At reranking, line windows with headers achieved the highest NDCG@10 (0.621), outperforming AST chunks with headers ($\Delta = -0.134$; exploratory, unadjusted $p = 0.0003$). While context headers provide a substantial boost for AST chunks ($\Delta = +0.146$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.001$), line windows without headers significantly outperform both AST chunks with headers ($\Delta = -0.093$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.0438$) and AST chunks without headers ($\Delta = -0.239$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.001$). 
Together with coverage and overlap audits (0.0\% fallback), this indicates that node-level AST extraction without sibling merging does not surpass sliding windows in requirement evidence retrieval. 
Our roadmap poses three open questions: (1)~\textit{Can graph-enriched headers bridge the information gap for fine-grained AST chunks?} (2)~\textit{How should code-RAG benchmarks weigh syntactic integrity against contextual density?} (3)~\textit{How can requirement retrieval best assist practitioners in verifying codebase capabilities?}
