Locating source code that evidences a technical requirement is fundamental in software analysis. While syntax-aware chunking is widely advocated to preserve code structure, prior evaluations conflate chunk boundaries with the contextual metadata prepended to chunks. In this Early Research Achievement (ERA) paper, we present a controlled 2 x 2 factorial evaluation crossing chunking strategy (node-level abstract syntax trees vs. 50-line windows) with context headers (present vs. absent). Evaluating across 25 job descriptions, 40 repositories, and three retrieval tiers (BM25, dense, cross-encoder reranking) against 882 human-labeled pairs, our findings show: (1) Context headers substantially boost AST chunks (NDCG@10 increases from 0.341 to 0.487, p < 0.001), while their gain for line windows is minor (Delta = +0.041, p = 0.2411). (2) Simple 50-line windows match or exceed AST chunking across all comparable tiers; even headerless line windows significantly outperform AST chunks with headers (Delta = -0.093, p = 0.0438). (3) Line windows with headers achieve the highest overall retrieval effectiveness (0.621 vs. 0.487, p = 0.0003). These results indicate that contextual density frequently outweighs syntactic boundary integrity in evidence retrieval; we conclude with a research roadmap on enriched headers and developer verification.
